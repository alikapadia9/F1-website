01

Hazard perception

Spotting what matters — a car about to pull out, a pedestrian about to step off — early enough to respond calmly instead of reacting late. This is one of the most common reasons for a fail, and one of the most trainable.

02

Road rules, precisely

Give-way lines, roundabouts, school zones, signage — the local detail that trips up even experienced drivers, especially if you've been driving under a different set of rules overseas.

03

Test-day confidence

Running the manoeuvres and routes you'll actually be assessed on until they're routine, so test day is a formality, not a surprise.

04

Logbook hours, done properly

If you need a fixed number of supervised hours, every one of them is logged correctly and spent on something useful — not just clocked.

How it works

The fast-track loop.

Three stages, repeated as needed. No wasted lessons.

Step 1

Assess

An honest read on where you're at — what's solid, what needs work, and how far you are from test-ready. No guessing.

Step 2

Correct

Targeted lessons on exactly the gaps that matter, in a dual-control car, with direct feedback after every drive.

Step 3

Certify

A final run-through against the real test standard, so test day confirms what you already know: you're ready.
